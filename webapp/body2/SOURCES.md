# SOURCES

Everything below was opened and checked on 2026-11-12. Nothing here is a clinical reference.

## Point names, codes, locations (361 points)
* **AcuAtlas 361-point dataset** - https://acupointatlas.com/atlas/ (JSON: https://acupointatlas.com/data/acupoints.json), **CC BY 4.0**, attribution: AcuAtlas.
  Used for: pinyin, hanzi, English name, meridian, point number, the English location sentence (`location`), category note, WHO page reference (`who_ref`).
  The location text is AcuAtlas's paraphrase of the WHO text, not WHO's own prose.
* **WHO Standard Acupuncture Point Locations in the Western Pacific Region (2008)** - https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/353407 (PDF, 249 p, 361 points). WHO publications are copyrighted, so the prototype does **not** ship WHO prose.
  Used only to *validate* AcuAtlas: the PDF text was parsed locally and every one of the 361 `code: Pinyin hanzi` headers compared.
  Result (`data/validation_report.json`): 361/361 codes found, 361/361 pinyin identical; hanzi differ only by traditional/simplified variants (5 headers). A crude word-overlap check on the location sentence flagged 7 points (BL61, BL2, KI6, BL5, BL7, GB13, CV24); All seven (BL61, BL2, KI6, BL5, BL7, GB13, CV24) were read by hand against the WHO text and mean the same thing, just worded differently (`hand_read_verdicts` in the report).
  The hand and finger points (LU7-11, LI1-6, PC5-9, HT4-9, SI1-6, TB1-6) and the foot points (ST41-45, SP1-5, LR1-4, KI1-6, BL60-67, GB40-44) and the back-of-head points (GV15-20, BL8-10, GB12, GB19, GB20) were placed by reading the WHO location sentence for each one; each placement's reasoning is in its `basis`. WHO prose is not reproduced.
* Code crosswalk: the repo / ATI uses `TB` (Triple Burner) and `LR` (Liver); WHO uses `TE` and `LR`; AcuAtlas uses `TE` and `LV`; Ren/Du are `CV`/`GV` in WHO. Each point's primary `code` is the repo style (`TB4`, `LR3`, `GV20`, `CV6`) so `POINT_KINDS` (keyed `LU9`, `TB4` ...) and `markMeridians` attach unchanged; all other spellings are in `alias` and are searchable (TE1, SJ1, LV3, DU20, REN6 ...).
* Yuan / Luo / Horary kinds: from the repo's `config/ati/meridian_points.json` (copied to `src_raw/repo_meridian_points.json`); at run time ATI's `lighting` payload overrides.

## Extra points (outside the 361)
* **Foot**: EX-LE8 Neihuaijian, EX-LE9 Waihuaijian, EX-LE10 Bafeng, EX-LE11 Duyin, EX-LE12 Qiduan. Codes follow the WHO extra-point nomenclature; location wording is paraphrased from a secondary reference list (the baike.duguji.cn extra-point page, found in the earlier research pass; I did not re-open it in this pass) - treat as unverified secondary sourcing. Positions are approximate, per `foot.json`.
* EX-UE11 Shixuan, EX-UE10 Sifeng, EX-UE9 Baxie: locations from the TCM Student "Ex Location" table (https://www.tcmstudent.com/study_tools/Ex%20Location.html) and tcmwindow.com hand-therapy list (http://www.tcmwindow.com/reflexology/handtherapy/acupoints/Common-acupoints-for-hand-therapy-(Part-5).shtml); the EX-UE9/10/11 codes are in the WHO extra-point nomenclature (https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/c4da2d29-c375-4cc2-a353-b736a90f7093/content, Annex 9). These are **secondary sources** - the WHO 2008 standard covers only the 361. The hanzi (十宣, 四缝, 八邪) are standard usage, not copied from those pages. No other extra points are included.

## Hand and foot art (second pass)
* **Hand template**: *Gray's Anatomy* (1918) fig. 1237, vector redraw `Gray1237 pl.svg` from Wikimedia Commons - **public domain**. Stored as `assets/source/Gray1237_pl.svg`. Skin outline, bones and palmar creases were traced from it (`tools/gray_base.py`, `tools/gray_hand.py`) and scaled to a typical 18.7 cm hand length; proportions are Gray's, absolute size is an assumption. Palm crease roles (distal transverse = heart line, proximal transverse = head line, thenar = life line) were identified by viewing the render.
* **Foot template (third pass)**: *Gray's Anatomy* (1918) fig. 834, vector redraw `Gray834 pl.svg` on Wikimedia Commons (Mysid, after M. Komorniczak) - **public domain**. Stored as `assets/source/Gray834_pl.svg`. The plantar outline was used for the heel, sole and medial/lateral borders and scaled to about 25 cm (`tools/gray_foot.py`); **the toes and web spaces were redrawn** (`tools/foot_skin.py`) because the diagram fuses toes 2-5 and has slit-like webs. Gray plates 268, 269 (bones of the foot, dorsal/plantar) and 290, 291 (medial/lateral views), public-domain scans of the 1918 edition, were used only as positional reference for the schematic bones and the side profiles (`src_raw/gray_foot_plates/`, not shipped). The bones are redrawn vectors, not traced; the side profiles are hand-drawn.
* **Foot proportions (third pass, round 2)**: overall proportions checked against two public-domain sources. (1) *Gray's Anatomy* (1918) fig. 834 and plates 268/269/290/291: ball-of-foot widest, medial arch concavity, hallux clearly the widest toe with toe 2 about level with it, toes 3-5 stepping back. (2) NASA RP-1024 *Anthropometric Source Book*, vol. 1 (1978, US Government work, public domain; https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19790003563, pp. III-62/63): foot length 24.1-27.0 cm and foot (ball) breadth 8.9-10.3 cm, i.e. breadth/length about 0.37-0.39; the drawn foot is 25.05 cm long and 9.6 cm wide at the ball (0.38). The NASA tables give no hallux or per-toe lengths, so the toe lengths, toe widths (hallux about 1.85x toe 2), the 0.95 cm arch concavity and the shallow web depths are my own estimates from the Gray figures and general anatomy, not measured values; they are approximate.
* **DBCLS bone drawings dropped.** The 2021 DBCLS foot-bone SVGs (Commons "202110 Dorsal/Plantar view of bones of right foot.svg", CC BY 4.0) were used in the second pass. Their Commons wikitext still carried the `{{LicenseReview}}` marker (licence not reviewed), so the foot no longer uses them; the files and any derived geometry are removed. The AcuAtlas figures are unchanged (CC BY 4.0, stated by their publisher).
* Everything else on the hand and foot (skin shading, nails, lunulae, creases other than Gray's, pads, tendon lines, toe shapes and clefts, malleoli, leg stubs, foot bones) is drawn here. Foot point placements assume 1 B-cun = 2.1 cm and 0.1 f-cun = 0.2 cm and are approximate (`approx: true`).
* **Side-view hand** (`tools/fix_side_hand.py`): hand-built parametric shape, no reference.
* **Back-of-head placements**: simple spherical-scalp model (`tools/build_points.py`), no reference drawing.

## Figure
* Silhouettes (front, back, side, face) are traced and smoothed from the AcuAtlas line-art figures (`/figures/*.png`, "original AcuAtlas diagrams ... CC BY 4.0"). Raw PNGs: `assets/source/`. The crude hands in the front/back figures are cut off at the wrist.
* The hand/foot templates are described above; the old `build_hand.py` hand was removed in the second pass. **Not a measured hand or foot.**
* Organs (lungs, heart, liver ...) are carried over from `webapp/body.html`'s own schematic `geometry.organs`, rescaled; approximate.
* Houses: twelve equal bands head-to-feet, as in the repo's reference image; region names verbatim from `config/ati/house_to_body_region.json`.

## Palmistry (traditional layer)
* Line and mount names checked against https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmistry (life, head, heart, fate lines; mounts Jupiter, Saturn, Apollo, Mercury, Mars positive/negative, Luna, Venus). Wikipedia describes palmistry as pseudoscience; interpretations conflict between schools.
* Line routes and mount positions are conventional drawings, placed by hand on the template (no precision claimed). The one-line meanings and the finger associations (index = Jupiter, middle = Saturn, ring = Apollo/Sun, little = Mercury) are common textbook conventions written in my own words, not quoted from one source.
