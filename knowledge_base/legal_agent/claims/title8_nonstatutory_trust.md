# "Title 8 organizational non-statutory trust" — EIN in 30 minutes

**Standing: UNSUPPORTED as stated.** Not because trusts are suspect — one part of
it is ordinary law — but because the load-bearing pieces are either misnamed or
prove nothing.

Run through the claim pipeline 2026-08-30: `prerequisite_missing`, confidence 0.0.

## What is actually true

**Non-statutory trusts are real.** A common-law express trust needs no statute to
exist — that is the whole subject of Chandler's *Express Trusts Under the Common
Law* (1912), which this system holds. Contrast a *statutory* trust like a
Delaware Statutory Trust, which exists because 12 Del. C. ch. 38 says it does.
Calling a trust "non-statutory" is a real distinction, not a magic word.

**Most trusts are not registered anywhere.** A private express trust generally
files nothing with a state. "Not public" is an accurate description of that fact.

## What does not hold

**"Title 8" does not correspond to anything.** Title 8 of the U.S. Code is
*Aliens and Nationality*. There is no trust provision in it. If the speaker meant
a different Title 8 — a state code, an internal form number — it has to be named,
because a citation nobody can open is not authority. This system checked: not in
the corpus, and no such provision is identifiable.

**The EIN proves nothing, and this is the crux.** The IRS issues EINs online and
by phone with essentially no substantive review — that is why it takes thirty
minutes. An EIN is an ACCOUNT NUMBER. It is not:

- a determination that the entity is validly formed
- a determination of how it will be classified for tax
- a determination that it is exempt from anything
- recognition of any theory about it

"The IRS gave me an EIN in thirty minutes" and "the IRS reviewed and accepted my
structure" are different sentences. The speed is offered as proof of acceptance
when it is evidence of the opposite: nobody looked.

**"Over-explaining" as a warning sign.** The advice to say little when applying
is presented as savvy. Read it the other way: if a full description of the
arrangement would cause the application to be refused or questioned, the
arrangement's viability depends on the agency not understanding it. That is not
a structure, it is a wager on inattention.

## What would defeat it even if the paperwork were perfect

- **Grantor trust rules, 26 U.S.C. §§ 671–679.** Held in this corpus. If the
  settlor keeps the levers — power to control beneficial enjoyment, administrative
  powers, revocation, income for the settlor — the income is taxed to the settlor
  however the deed is labelled. Substance over form, codified for trusts.
- **Economic substance, 26 U.S.C. § 7701(o).** Conjunctive test, and the penalty
  under § 6662(b)(6) is strict liability: § 6664(c)(2) removes the reasonable-
  cause defence. No opinion letter cures it.
- **Self-settled trust doctrine.** A settlor cannot be his own beneficiary and his
  own shield. Creditors reach the ceiling of what the trustee could distribute.
- **The IRS publishes this as a known scheme family** — "abusive trust
  arrangements", the pure trust / unincorporated business organisation cluster.
  A structure whose promoters emphasise the EIN and discourage explanation sits
  squarely in it.

## The honest position

A private express trust is a real instrument with real uses — succession,
management for a beneficiary who cannot manage for themselves, holding property
across a family. Those uses do not require a special name, and they do not turn
on how quickly an EIN was issued.

If someone wants this tested rather than believed, the pipeline needs: the actual
instrument, the jurisdiction, the specific provision said to authorise it, and
what happened afterwards — the four things `claim_open` asks for and this video
supplies none of.
