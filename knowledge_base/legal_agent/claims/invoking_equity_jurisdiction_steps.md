# Claimed steps to "invoke equity jurisdiction"

**Stance: ADVOCACY — evaluated.** A seven-step filing procedure produced by a
general-purpose chatbot and supplied by the principal on 2026-08-28. Recorded
here as a CLAIM WITH ITS EVALUATION ATTACHED, not as procedure to follow. Some
steps rest on real doctrine; several would get a filing rejected, and two are
recognised markers that draw adverse attention to everything filed with them.

**Source:** screenshot of a ChatGPT session, "amyr-law" thread, supplied by the
principal. Not authority. No court, statute or rule is cited in it.

**This is analysis, not legal advice.** Every point below is checkable against
the rules and the case law; check it before relying on any of it.

---

## The central defect

The steps assume equity is a **separate forum you gain entry to by ritual**.
It is not, and has not been in federal court since 1938. One form of action
exists - the civil action - and law and equity are administered together. Most
states did the same decades ago.

Equity today is a set of **remedies you plead facts to earn**: injunction,
specific performance, constructive trust, accounting, rescission, equitable
lien, subrogation. They are live and powerful. The route to them is pleading
the elements in an ordinary complaint, not requesting admission to a chancery
side that no longer exists in most courts.

Delaware is the notable exception - it has a real Court of Chancery, and the
Delaware Statutory Trust Act in `reference/legal/` is its statute. Even there,
a case opens with a complaint, not an appointment.

## Step by step

| # | Step claimed | Standing |
|---|--------------|----------|
| 1 | Appointment with Clerk or Master in Chancery | **Wrong.** No separate equity docket to be admitted to. A Master in Chancery is a judicial officer assigned to a case, not an intake desk. |
| 2 | "Bill in Equity" or "Affidavit of Status" | **Outdated / not a filing.** "Bill in equity" was superseded by "complaint". "Affidavit of Status" is not a recognised pleading. |
| 3 | Statement of No Adequate Remedy at Law | **Has a real core.** Inadequacy of the legal remedy IS an element of most equitable relief. But it is pleaded *inside* the complaint, tied to facts - not filed as a separate document. |
| 4 | Demand the case be sealed | **Cannot be demanded.** Sealing requires a motion overcoming a strong presumption of public access, granted on findings. A demand without that showing is denied. |
| 5 | Require an Article III judge, private chambers | **Not available.** A party cannot select the judge. State courts have no Article III judges at all, so in a state matter this is incoherent. |
| 6 | Pay fees in "lawful money or silver tender" | **Recognised marker.** Courts take ordinary payment. This flags the filing and colours how everything else in it is read. |
| 7 | Notice your status and fiduciary role | **The error itself.** Declaring a status does not change how a court treats the matter. |

## What the corpus already says about step 7

The private-ordering commentary in `knowledge_base/legal_agent/transcripts/`
is written expressly against this move. Its thesis: private ordering is a
permission the law grants, not an exemption from it, and the recurring error is
"the attempt to assert private rights inside a publicly regulated space on the
theory that the private character of the parties or the instrument removes the
activity from regulation." Its warning about people who believe otherwise:
they "will misuse it, lose, and often pay penalties for the attempt."

Step 7 is that error stated as a filing instruction.

## What to do instead, if the goal is equitable protection

Name the remedy and plead its elements. If the principal's equitable interest
in a specific asset is the concern, the doctrines that actually reach it are
constructive trust, equitable lien, subrogation, accounting and specific
performance - each with its own elements, each available in an ordinary civil
action. That is the analysis worth building: which remedy fits the facts, and
whether the elements can be pleaded.
