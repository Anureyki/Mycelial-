# Canons of contract construction

## Reading the instrument

- **Four corners** - the agreement is construed from within its own text first.
- **Intent of the parties** as objectively manifested in the words used, not
  as privately held.
- **Plain and ordinary meaning** for undefined terms; technical terms carry
  their technical meaning in the relevant trade.
- **Defined terms control.** A capitalised or expressly defined term means what
  the agreement says, regardless of general usage.
- **Whole-instrument** - construe clauses so they are consistent with one
  another. Avoid a reading that nullifies another provision.
- **Specific provisions govern general ones**; a negotiated rider or
  handwritten term generally prevails over pre-printed boilerplate.

## Where the text does not settle it

- **Contra proferentem** - ambiguity is construed against the party that drafted
  the instrument. Weight increases sharply where bargaining power was unequal or
  the document is an adhesion contract offered on a take-it-or-leave-it basis.
- **Parol evidence rule** - where a writing is a complete and final expression
  (an integrated agreement), prior or contemporaneous understandings do not vary
  its terms. Note whether an integration/merger clause is present.
- **Course of performance**, then course of dealing, then usage of trade, as
  interpretive aids in that order of weight.

## Formation elements to identify separately

Offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, mutual assent. Recording that these
elements are PRESENT in the text is extraction. Deciding whether they are
legally SUFFICIENT is not this agent's task.

## Signals worth flagging to the principal

Not conclusions - observations that deserve human attention:

- an obligation that runs one way with no corresponding consideration
- a term permitting unilateral amendment by one party
- a fee, charge, or interest that accrues to a party other than the one bearing
  the risk
- an integration clause paired with representations made only outside the writing
- a party signing in one capacity while benefiting in another
- assignment of a right without assignment of the corresponding duty
